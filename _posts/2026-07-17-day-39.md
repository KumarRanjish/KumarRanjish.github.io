what_i_learned: |
   Today was another major milestone in my summer research journey. I started writing
   my research paper in Overleaf by organizing all the work I have completed throughout
   my AI-driven Alzheimer's disease drug discovery project into a scientific manuscript.

   I learned how to structure a research paper by writing the Introduction,
   Methodology, Results, Discussion, and Conclusion sections in a logical order.
   While writing, I carefully reviewed my complete research workflow, including
   dataset preparation, data cleaning, QSAR modeling, external validation, molecular
   docking, and result analysis. This helped me better understand how every stage
   of the project connects together to answer the research question.

   Another important lesson I learned today was that conducting research is only
   one part of the scientific process. Communicating the research clearly through
   a well-written manuscript is equally important because it allows other researchers
   to understand, reproduce, and build upon the work. Writing the paper also helped
   me identify small details that I had overlooked and improved my overall understanding
   of the project.

blockers: |
   The biggest challenge today was organizing several weeks of research into a
   well-structured scientific manuscript. I had to carefully review my previous
   notes, results, and workflow to ensure that every section accurately reflected
   the work I completed. Writing in a clear scientific style while keeping the
   language simple and easy to understand also required extra attention.

reflection: |
   Today gave me the opportunity to reflect on everything I have accomplished
   during this summer research internship. Looking through my previous work made
   me realize how much I have learned over the past several weeks. From learning
   AI-based QSAR modeling and molecular docking to now writing a complete research
   paper, I can clearly see my growth as a researcher. Starting the manuscript in
   Overleaf made me feel proud because it represents the final stage of turning my
   research into something that can be shared with the scientific community. This
   experience has strengthened both my scientific writing skills and my confidence
   in conducting computational drug discovery research for Alzheimer's disease.
