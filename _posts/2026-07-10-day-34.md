what_i_learned: |
   Today I focused on preparing the Tau dataset for the external validation phase of our QSAR modeling workflow. The primary goal was to ensure that the external validation dataset was completely independent from the internal dataset that had been used for model training and internal validation. This is one of the most important steps in machine learning because it allows the model to be evaluated on new, unseen compounds and provides a more realistic measure of its predictive performance.

   I began by comparing the cleaned Tau dataset with the Tau descriptor dataset that will be used for external validation. To prevent data leakage, I carefully compared the molecular structures of compounds in both datasets and identified any overlapping molecules. Every compound that was already present in the internal dataset was removed from the external dataset so that only unique compounds remained for testing. This ensures that the external validation results are unbiased and accurately reflect the model's ability to generalize to completely new chemical structures.

   After removing the overlapping compounds, I verified that the final external dataset contained only unique molecules. I also checked that all required molecular descriptors were available and correctly formatted so the dataset would be compatible with the trained QSAR models. These verification steps are essential because missing descriptors or duplicate compounds can affect the quality and reliability of the validation results.

   In addition to the dataset preparation, I also spent time preparing for my upcoming research presentation. I reviewed the complete QSAR workflow, including data collection, dataset cleaning, feature preparation, model development, internal validation, and the importance of external validation. I organized my presentation materials, reviewed the key concepts that I will explain, and practiced presenting the workflow in a clear and logical order. This preparation helped me better understand the overall research process and improved my confidence in explaining my work to others.
