what_i_learned: |
   Today was one of the most important milestones in our Alzheimer's disease drug discovery project as I completed the QSAR modeling workflow for my assigned
   BACE1 dataset. The day began with carefully reviewing my entire notebook to ensure that every preprocessing and modeling step followed the project
   requirements before building the final machine learning models.

   I first revisited the cleaned BACE1 dataset and verified that duplicate compounds, conflicting activity records, and unreliable entries had been properly
   handled. After confirming the quality of the dataset, I checked the final active and inactive class distribution to determine whether balanced machine
   learning models would provide any improvement over the regular baseline models.

   After preparing the molecular fingerprints, I developed both classification and regression QSAR models. For the classification task, I trained and compared
   multiple machine learning algorithms, including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Extra Trees, Histogram Gradient Boosting, and their balanced versions,
   along with the Balanced Random Forest model. Instead of relying only on accuracy, I evaluated each model using Balanced Accuracy, MCC, Active Recall,
   Inactive Recall, ROC-AUC, and PR-AUC to obtain a more complete understanding of model performance.

   Among all the classification models, the Regular Histogram Gradient Boosting model achieved the best overall performance with an accuracy of 85.76%,
   Balanced Accuracy of 85.77%, MCC of 0.7149, Active Recall of 86.11%, Inactive Recall of 85.43%, ROC-AUC of 0.9320, and PR-AUC of 0.9166. These results
   demonstrated that the model was able to accurately distinguish active and inactive BACE1 inhibitors.

   Since the dataset also contained pIC50 values, I treated BACE1 as a regression problem to predict compound potency. I trained Ridge Regression, Random
   Forest Regressor, Extra Trees Regressor, and Histogram Gradient Boosting Regressor, and compared their performance using RMSE, MAE, R², and Spearman
   Correlation.

   The Random Forest Regressor achieved the best regression performance with an RMSE of 0.69996, MAE of 0.49345, R² of 0.73861, and a Spearman Correlation
   of 0.85414. These results showed that the model could predict pIC50 values with good accuracy while also preserving the ranking of compound potency.

   Finally, I combined the outputs from the best classification and regression models to generate the final prediction table for the BACE1 dataset. Each
   compound now includes its predicted active or inactive class, probability of activity, predicted pIC50 value, and ranking. This completed the QSAR
   modeling stage and prepared the highest-ranked compounds for the next phase of our research, which will involve molecular docking and biological validation.
