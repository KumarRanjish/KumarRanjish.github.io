what_i_learned: |
   Today marked an important transition in our Alzheimer's disease drug discovery project as we began preparing for the next stage of our workflow:
   molecular docking and structural validation of the compounds identified through our machine learning models.To establish a strong foundation before
   entering the docking phase, we first conducted an in-depth review of relevant research papers related to machine learning, QSAR modeling, and molecular 
   docking in Alzheimer's disease research. These literature reviews helped us understand how researchers integrate different computational approaches to 
   identify promising drug candidates and validate their interactions with biological targets.One of the papers we reviewed focused on combining ensemble
   machine learning models with fibril docking to discover novel tau aggregation inhibitors. Through this study, we learned how multiple machine learning
   models can be combined to improve prediction accuracy and how docking can be used as an additional validation step after compound screening.Since our 
   project is now moving toward molecular docking, understanding these methodologies was particularly important. The paper reviews helped us connect our 
   current QSAR and machine learning workflows with the structure-based validation techniques that we will soon begin using.Following the literature review,
   we focused on learning the fundamentals of molecular docking through educational YouTube videos and explanations provided by our mentor. We learned about
   important concepts such as proteins, ligands, binding pockets, docking scores, binding affinity, and protein-ligand interactions. Our mentor also explained
   how docking serves as a bridge between computational predictions and potential biological activity.One of the most interesting concepts introduced today was
   the lock-and-key analogy, where the protein acts as a lock and the compound acts as a key. Molecular docking attempts to determine how well the key fits into
   the lock and estimates the strength of the interaction between them.Towards the end of the session, our mentor assigned us responsibilities related to the 
   upcoming molecular docking workflow. These assignments will allow us to gain hands-on experience with structure-based drug discovery methods and contribute 
   to the next phase of our Alzheimer's disease research project.
