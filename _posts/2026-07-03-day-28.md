what_i_learned: |
   Today was another insightful and productive day in our Alzheimer's disease drug discovery research project as our team continued preparing for the upcoming
   molecular docking phase while also expanding our understanding of artificial intelligence techniques used in modern drug discovery.

   We began the session by conducting detailed reviews of several research papers related to molecular docking, machine learning, deep learning, and computational
   drug discovery. These literature reviews helped us understand how researchers integrate ligand-based approaches such as QSAR modeling with structure-based
   approaches such as molecular docking to identify and validate potential therapeutic compounds.

   An important part of today's learning involved exploring different molecular docking software commonly used in computational biology and drug discovery research.
   We learned about various docking platforms, their applications, strengths, and limitations, as well as how researchers choose appropriate software depending
   on their target proteins, ligands, and computational requirements. Understanding these tools provided valuable insight into the practical aspects of
   structure-based drug discovery workflows.

   In addition to docking software, we spent time watching educational videos and tutorials focused on deep learning and neural networks. These sessions helped us
   understand the fundamental principles behind artificial intelligence models that are capable of learning complex patterns from large biological datasets.
   We learned how neural networks are inspired by the structure of the human brain and how deep learning models can automatically learn hierarchical features
   from molecular and biological data without requiring extensive manual feature engineering.

   We also explored how deep learning techniques are increasingly being integrated into computational drug discovery for applications such as molecular property
   prediction, virtual screening, protein structure prediction, and drug-target interaction analysis. Learning about these technologies helped us appreciate
   the growing role of artificial intelligence in accelerating biomedical research and therapeutic discovery.

   Another valuable activity today was participating in peer review sessions with fellow researchers. During these discussions, we shared our progress, exchanged
   ideas, reviewed each other's understanding of the concepts, and provided constructive feedback on our approaches and interpretations. The peer review process
   allowed us to learn from different perspectives and improve our understanding of complex research topics.

   By the end of the session, we had developed a stronger understanding of both traditional molecular docking methods and emerging deep learning approaches that
   are shaping the future of computational drug discovery and Alzheimer's disease research.
