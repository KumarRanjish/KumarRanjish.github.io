what_i_learned: |
   Today, I continued working on my Alzheimer's disease drug discovery project by focusing on the Tau protein. I used a cleaned Tau-related compound dataset
   and first explored the data to understand its structure and important features. After selecting molecular descriptors such as MW, LogP, TPSA, HBD, and HBA,
   I prepared the dataset for QSAR modeling and created a teaching label based on activity and drug-likeness criteria.Next, I split the dataset into training
   and testing sets and built several machine learning models, including Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Logistic Regression. I then evaluated their performance 
   using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrices. While some models achieved very high accuracy, I found that the dataset 
   was highly imbalanced, with only a few promising compounds compared to a large number of non-promising compounds. This helped me better understand the strengths 
   and limitations of different machine learning models when working with real-world biological data.

   I learned how to build and compare different QSAR machine learning models and evaluate their performance using various metrics. I gained a better understanding 
   of confusion matrices, precision, recall, and F1-scores, and learned why accuracy alone can sometimes be misleading. I also learned that different machine
   learning algorithms can produce different results even when trained on the same dataset. Most importantly, I realized how class imbalance can significantly 
   affect model performance and why it is important to look beyond accuracy when evaluating predictive models.
