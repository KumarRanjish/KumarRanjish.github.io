what_i_learned: |
   Today I started learning the molecular docking workflow for my Alzheimer's disease research project. I first studied the overall docking pipeline and understood how molecular docking is used as a structure-based validation method after QSAR and ChemBERTa candidate selection. I learned that the docking study will focus on the acetylcholinesterase (AChE) protein because it is the primary target for the selected compounds.

   I explored the Protein Data Bank (RCSB PDB) and searched for human acetylcholinesterase crystal structures. After comparing different structures, I selected PDB ID 7E3H because it is a high-quality human AChE crystal structure solved by X-ray crystallography and contains the co-crystallized inhibitor donepezil. I learned why experimentally determined protein structures are preferred over predicted structures such as AlphaFold 3 or Protenix whenever they are available.

   I downloaded the 7E3H protein structure and opened it in ChimeraX for structural inspection. During this process, I learned how to identify the protein chains, the co-crystallized ligand (Donepezil/E20), glycans, and missing residues. I also learned that the co-crystallized ligand is important because it identifies the active binding site and will later be used for docking validation through reference ligand redocking.

   I also explored different molecular docking software and compared AutoDock Vina, Meeko, ChimeraX, and PyRx. After understanding the advantages and disadvantages of each software, I decided to use PyRx because it provides an easy graphical interface while using the AutoDock Vina docking engine internally. I successfully installed PyRx and imported the selected AChE protein into the software, preparing the environment for the next stage of receptor preparation and docking.

blockers: |
