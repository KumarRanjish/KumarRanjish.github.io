what_i_learned: |
   Today I completed my first AlphaFold 3 protein–ligand docking demonstration as part of our Alzheimer's disease drug discovery project.
   The session began by selecting the BACE1 protein because it is an important therapeutic target for Alzheimer's disease. I also selected
   Resveratrol as the ligand for this demonstration and learned how to obtain its canonical SMILES from PubChem and the protein sequence
   from UniProt.

   After preparing both the protein sequence and ligand information, I submitted the job through Protenix (AlphaFold 3). Once the prediction
   finished successfully, I downloaded the predicted protein–ligand complex and opened it in UCSF ChimeraX for visualization.

   During the ChimeraX session, I learned how to open the predicted CIF file, identify the protein and ligand, use different visualization
   styles such as cartoon and stick representations, and examine the predicted binding region. I also learned how to use the contact analysis
   command to identify interactions between the ligand and nearby protein residues. The analysis showed that the ligand was positioned inside
   a pocket-like region of the BACE1 protein with multiple predicted contacts, making the result visually reasonable for a docking demonstration.

   Finally, I prepared a short docking report summarizing the target protein, ligand information, tools used, prediction results,
   interaction analysis, interpretation, limitations, and the overall learning from the docking workflow.

blockers: |
   Since this was my first time using ChimeraX, I initially had difficulty finding the command line and understanding the correct commands
   for viewing the ligand and analyzing protein–ligand interactions. I also spent time learning how AlphaFold 3 predictions differ from
   traditional molecular docking and how to interpret the predicted complex correctly.

reflection: |
   Today's session gave me a practical understanding of AI-based protein–ligand structure prediction and molecular visualization.
   I learned that generating a prediction is only one part of the workflow, and it is equally important to visualize the complex,
   examine the ligand position, and understand the predicted interactions. This experience has helped me better understand the structural
   validation step of computational drug discovery and will be valuable when I analyze the top BACE1 compounds predicted from my QSAR models
   in the next stage of the project.
