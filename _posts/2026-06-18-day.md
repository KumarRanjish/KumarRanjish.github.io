what_i_learned: |
   Today was a very informative and productive day in the AI Summer Research Institute. We attended an important lecture on Artificial Intelligence, 
   . We were also introduced to Overleaf and learned how it can be used for writing professional research papers, reports, and collaborative scientific
   documents using LaTeX. In our Alzheimer's disease drug discovery project, a major part of the day was spent understanding the challenges associated
   with our Tau protein dataset, particularly the issue of class imbalance, where one category of compounds contains significantly more samples than the
   other. We learned that relying only on model accuracy can be misleading when working with imbalanced datasets and that additional evaluation metrics
   such as precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrices are necessary for properly assessing model performance. We also discussed several techniques
   for addressing class imbalance, including oversampling with SMOTE, undersampling the majority class, and using class-weighted machine learning algorithms.
   In addition, we continued exploring the Tau protein dataset and reviewed important molecular descriptors used in our QSAR models, including Molecular Weight 
   (MW), LogP, TPSA, Hydrogen Bond Donors (HBD), and Hydrogen Bond Acceptors (HBA), and discussed their role in predicting compound activity and drug-likeness. 
   Overall, today's activities helped me gain a deeper understanding of machine learning model evaluation, data preprocessing, and the importance of properly
   balanced datasets when developing reliable AI models for Alzheimer's disease drug discovery.
