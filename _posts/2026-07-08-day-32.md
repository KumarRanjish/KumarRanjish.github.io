what_i_learned: |
   Today’s research session focused on understanding the molecular docking stage that will follow our machine learning and QSAR modeling workflow. We began
   by exploring and reviewing several widely used molecular docking software packages used in computational drug discovery. During the discussion, we compared
   different docking tools, their workflows, strengths, and common applications in structure-based drug discovery. We also learned how these software
   packages predict how small drug-like molecules bind to a target protein and how docking scores and binding poses help researchers identify promising
   therapeutic candidates.

   Along with reviewing the software, we discussed how molecular docking complements our AI-driven virtual screening pipeline. We learned that after machine
   learning identifies potential compounds, molecular docking provides structural validation by estimating how well those compounds interact with the target
   protein. This helped us better understand how different computational techniques work together throughout the Alzheimer's disease drug discovery process.

   After the morning research session, we took a break and participated in a team-building game designed to help everyone get to know each other better.
   The activity encouraged communication, collaboration, and interaction among the research participants, making it easier to build stronger connections
   within the team. It was a fun experience that reminded us that successful research also depends on effective teamwork and communication.

   Later in the session, we watched and discussed *The Mercy* movie. Through the discussion, we reflected on themes such as perseverance, commitment,
   resilience, and decision-making under challenging circumstances. We talked about how these qualities are valuable not only in everyday life but also in
   scientific research, where persistence, patience, and continuous learning are essential for overcoming obstacles and achieving long-term goals.
