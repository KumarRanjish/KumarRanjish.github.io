what_i_learned: |
   Today was an informative and productive day. In the first session, we attended a workshop about data analysis. During the workshop,
   we learned how data is prepared and used before building a machine learning model. We discussed why it is important to understand the 
   dataset properly before training any model.One of the important topics we learned was how training and testing work in machine learning.
   I learned that the dataset is usually divided into two parts: training data and testing data. The training data is used to teach the model, 
   while the testing data is used to check how well the model performs on new data. This helped me understand why we should not use the same data 
   for both training and testing, because it can give misleading results.We also learned about different evaluation metrics used to check
   model performance. Some of the metrics discussed were accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrix, and ROC-AUC. I understood that 
   accuracy alone is not always enough, especially when the dataset is imbalanced. Other metrics are also important because they show how well the 
   model identifies active and inactive compounds.After the break, I worked on my own BACE1 dataset for the QSAR project. I focused on correcting the 
   feedback given for my dataset submission. I reviewed the dataset step by step and worked on fixing the issues in my Google Colab notebook. I checked
   the original BACE1 dataset size, activity values, pIC50 values, SMILES columns, and activity labels.I also worked on fixing the final cleaned CSV
   file. I learned that the cleaned dataset must have clear and correct column names, such as Compound_ID, Standardized_SMILES, Canonical_SMILES, InChIKey,
   pIC50, Activity_Label, QC_Flag, and molecular descriptors. I also learned that the final files should be saved with the exact filenames requested by the mentor.
   Another important part of my work was reviewing duplicate compounds and uncertain records. I worked on identifying duplicate structures using InChIKey,
   checking BindingDB conflict records, reviewing high-variability records, and identifying activity label conflicts using the provisional pIC50 ≥ 7 threshold.
   I learned that these records should not be removed without review, but they should be clearly flagged and documented.Overall, today’s workshop helped me understand 
   the basic ideas of data analysis, training and testing, and model evaluation. After the break, I applied those ideas to my own BACE1 dataset. This helped me 
  understand that preparing clean and well-documented data is a very important step before starting QSAR modeling.
